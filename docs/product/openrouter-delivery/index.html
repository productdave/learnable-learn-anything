1. Product definition

Problem and opportunity

Today, a learner can finish setting up a course and still face two provider connections, two balances and provider-specific errors. Several recovery and account surfaces assume an Anthropic key; images assume OpenAI; the tutor has another key path. Replacing the transport alone leaves that experience intact. The product should take responsibility for access, cost, progress and recovery while the learner focuses on what they want to learn.

Who it is for

Primary: an invited learner or creator who has a learning goal, optionally has notes, URLs or files, and wants a usable private course draft. Secondary: a returning creator with an older course funded by their own provider connections. Operator: David, who grants complimentary pilot credits, controls total provider spending and investigates uncertain charges. Shared teams, institutional accounts and anonymous paid generation are outside this pilot.

Job to be done

When I have a topic or source material, help me turn it into a course at a cost I understand. Let me review the direction before the full draft is written, show me what is saved, and let me continue safely if something stops.

First release scope

Invite-only, non-commercial web pilot on the current Hobby target. New eligible jobs use Learnable-funded OpenRouter access and complimentary Learnable credits. Reuse Goal, Experience, Context and Review; include course outline and research approvals, selected learning materials, purposeful illustrations, private saved drafts, durable progress, cost estimates, cancellation, recovery and usage history. Use a small enforced pilot course-size envelope; the first acceptance fixture is one module with three lessons. Define the actual cohort size and course cap before enabling the pilot.

Later scope

Selling credit bundles, setting real prices or markup, checkout, refunds of money, subscriptions, public distribution, ChatGPT/Slack entry points, team billing, learner-facing model switches and a full human refinement workflow. The credit architecture should support later sales without pretending that complimentary pilot credits are money or raw provider tokens.

Tutor and editing boundary

Recommended pilot default: course creation is funded; in-course AI tutor and AI rewrite proposals are unavailable to platform-funded accounts until they have their own funded routes and limits. Remove their key prompts for that cohort and enforce the same denial server-side. Reading, quizzes, flashcards, checklists and ordinary saved learning progress remain usable. Older creator-funded jobs keep a clearly labelled legacy connection path. If tutor is included, add F-10 before integrated acceptance; do not quietly retain BYOK as the solution.

Business hypothesis

A learner will prefer one predictable Learnable balance to multiple provider accounts, and a task-specific model mix can lower course costs while keeping the draft useful. Validate the experience and whole-course contribution before choosing a bundle size or dollar price. The earlier idea of 500 tokens becomes a possible bundle of Learnable credits; one credit must never be presented as one model token.

How credits should work

Proposed product rules. Complimentary pilot credits and future purchased credits are distinct from the provider-dollar ledger. The prototype amounts are illustrative.

MomentRule
TerminologyLearnable credits are a product unit. Tokens and USD are internal provider accounting units. The pilot uses complimentary credits with no stated cash value.
Before workA quote specifies the setup/source revision, scope, expiry, maximum credit debit and operator-approved provider-dollar envelope. Quoting and viewing sources do not buy an AI response.
Available / held / usedAvailable = granted minus held minus settled debits. A hold lowers available credits but is not yet a charge. Display all three without double-counting. Two devices must see the same authoritative balance.
Proposed debit policyDebit only valid outputs durably saved and made available to the learner: outline, research, lessons and accepted images. User-requested revisions are newly quoted work. Do not charge the learner again for duplicate operations, invalid model output or infrastructure repair. Learnable still records and pays every known provider charge.
Calculation to implement after policy sign-offUse a versioned conversion policy: cumulative credits = round upward once on aggregate eligible cost × pricing multiplier ÷ configured credit value. Debit only the difference from prior settlement, up to the learner-authorized cap. Lock the version to the authorization. No dollar or multiplier values are approved here.
Partial / cancellationKeep and itemize valid saved work. Settle only those delivered units under the proposed policy; release unstarted work. A dispatched request may finish after Cancel. Wait for its known outcome before releasing the associated hold. Cancellation is not a promise of zero cost.
Unknown outcomeKeep the provider reservation and the matching learner hold pending; block blind replacement. Show 'Checking a charge' and saved work. An operator reconciles against a provider receipt; any courtesy adjustment is an explicit ledger entry, not deletion of cost evidence.
Long human reviewRelease holds for unstarted future stages when the current authorization expires. Keep consumed credits and unresolved in-flight holds. On return, quote remaining work again and obtain consent if the price, scope or maximum changes. Never discard the reviewed outline or research.
Price / model changePreserve the immutable job identity and funding source; append a new explicit authorization revision for remaining work. No silent model substitution or cap increase. A retired or unavailable pinned model pauses the affected stage until the approved change path is accepted.
Example onlyPrototype numbers are synthetic: 500 complimentary credits, estimate 50–80, maximum hold 80, final debit 62, release 18. These are not prices, promises or a cost forecast.
Commercial laterMeasure provider spend, unsuccessful attempts, hosting, payment costs and support before selecting margins or bundles. The provider ledger cannot substitute for customer credit or monetary refund accounting.

Design direction

One clear action at each step

Keep the existing Goal → Experience → Context → Review sequence. Add an understandable cost-and-access confirmation instead of provider setup. Use familiar course cards and private reader views. Put detailed usage in Account; show the current maximum and saved work where the learner makes a decision.

Navigation and hierarchy

Your Courses, Create course, and Account & credits remain the primary destinations. Quote confirmation, review and recovery each have one primary action. Model IDs, tokens, policy hashes and operator controls stay outside the learner flow.

Components and copy

Use the current app's calm typography, rounded cards and semantic status colors. Label statuses with words and icons. Use “Available”, “Held for this course” and “Used”; explain holds next to the value. Error copy says what happened, what is retained and the next safe action.

Responsive and accessible

Single-column mobile layout; no hidden financial totals. Minimum 44px controls, visible focus, semantic headings, associated labels and polite status announcements. Restore focus to the changed heading or error. Support keyboard, screen readers, reduced motion and 200% text.

Open the interactive experience prototype Synthetic data only. No real generation or balance changes.

The delivery loop and release gate

GateRequired work
Before implementationEach feature has a user outcome, included/excluded states, data/credit rules, API boundary, UI copy, dependencies and specific acceptance tests. Review the relevant prototype states first.
During implementationBuild the smallest usable vertical slice on the isolated branch. Reuse accepted infrastructure; don't expose a half-wired paid route. Add meaningful regression tests for ownership, money and recovery.
Local feature QARun targeted unit/contracts, actual local DB tests when accounting changes, real endpoint integration, browser interaction and accessible/mobile states relevant to that feature. Test both platform and legacy modes.
Evidence and defectsRecord command/build/commit, fixture, expected/actual result, screenshots where useful, cost/reservations and unresolved defects. Distinguish code complete, local QA passed, live check passed and hosted accepted.
Move to the next featureResolve blockers and accept the current feature's defined local slice before starting the next implementation. If a required live dependency is unavailable, record the block; documentation/design may continue, but don't mark the feature accepted.
Integrated QAAfter feature QA, test the complete journey, cross-feature races, long pauses, old clients, account switch, operator reconciliation and partial recovery against a fresh staging package.
Paid QAOne logical run shares the US$5 cap across text, research/search, images, reasoning, corrections and parallel calls. Estimate and reserve first; reduce fixtures or stop if the next dispatch cannot fit. No auto-reload or reopening closed runs.
Release laterKeep release candidate, staging acceptance and production authorization separate. Verify exact source/artifact including concurrent recovery fixes, migration/rollback plan, cohort settings, provider access and accounting totals. Merge/deploy only after explicit release instruction.

A feature is not “done” because its code exists. Record local QA, required live checks and hosted acceptance separately. A staging pass does not authorize a production release.

How we will judge the result

MeasureProposed acceptance target
Access friction100% of tested new-pilot routes are free of provider key prompts; at least 4 of 5 invited usability participants complete setup/quote explanation without help. Proposed target; not measured.
Money correctnessZero duplicate debits, cross-owner access, negative available balance or provider-envelope overruns in the defined concurrency/fault suite. All dispatched operations have a reservation and settled or unresolved receipt state.
Draft qualityScore accuracy, attribution, coverage, usefulness and assessment correctness on representative fixtures. No critical factual/safety/citation/answer defect is acceptable; target at least 4/5 on each rubric dimension. Human-reviewed live results required.
RecoveryEvery prescribed recovery fixture retains prior accepted work and has no paid replay of an unknown operation. Review pauses are distinguishable from stalls.
Usability and accessibilityComplete the happy path and recovery with keyboard, screen-reader spot checks, narrow viewport and 200% text. Every error explains retained work and a safe next action.
Performance and economicsMeasure setup-to-outline, active generation time excluding human review, saved-draft completion, provider cost by stage, absorbed failures, credit debit and support effort. Establish latency and commercial targets from evidence; no invented speed or savings claim.

Changes the existing technical plan needs

GapRequired change
Separate credits from dollarsThe existing SQL ledger is provider-cost infrastructure, not a customer wallet. Add a distinct credit ledger and atomic link between quote consent, holds and eligible debits; do not relabel micro-USD as credits.
Fix long-review expiryCurrent policy snapshots expire within 24 hours and the new migration makes the binding immutable. Before F-02, separate immutable funding/route identity from append-only quote/authorization revisions. Test multi-day reviews, expiry and new consent; do not simply extend timestamps in place.
Cover the actual entry pointsBoth /api/setups/generate and /api/gen/start create jobs. Review/resume/restart/sweep/credentials-ready and image requests must share the resolver. Tutor and proposal routes require explicit scope handling.
Preserve current reliability workThe OpenRouter branch has the Designer rationale baseline but not all newer production timeout/credit-message changes. Reconcile those reviewed changes before relevant feature integration and packaging. Never copy private production records or frozen output into the PR.

Product evidence firstThe visible credit policy, cohort/exclusion copy and long-review rules guide the implementation. Do not let the current adapter shape silently define the customer promise.
